India's computer emergency response team, CERT-In, issued directions in April 2022 under the Information Technology Act that apply to service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations. Two requirements catch teams out: reporting specified types of cyber incidents within six hours of noticing them, and maintaining certain system logs for a defined period within India.

**Why it matters for Indian businesses.** Six hours is shorter than most companies' internal approval chains. If the person who can authorise a report is travelling, or if nobody knows which incidents qualify, the clock runs out while people debate. The incident categories listed in the directions include events such as data breaches, ransomware-type compromises and attacks on critical systems, so the rule is not limited to large enterprises.

**Our take.** The fix is mostly preparation. Decide in advance who may file, what a qualifying incident looks like, which log sources prove what happened and where reporting contact details live. Test it in a tabletop exercise. Align the same runbook with your obligations under the DPDP Act to notify the Data Protection Board and affected individuals of personal data breaches, so teams do not run two panicked processes at once.

**What to do this week.** Read the directions' list of reportable incidents, check that your log retention matches, name a deputy for the person who files and run a thirty-minute walkthrough.

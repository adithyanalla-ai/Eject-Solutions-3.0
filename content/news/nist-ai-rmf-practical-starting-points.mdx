The US National Institute of Standards and Technology published its AI Risk Management Framework as a voluntary guide for organisations that design, develop, deploy or use AI. It organises work into four functions: Govern, Map, Measure and Manage. Although it is American, global parents, customers and auditors increasingly use it as a reference.

**Why it matters for Indian businesses.** If you serve US or European customers, or work for a multinational's capability centre in Hyderabad, expect questions that echo this framework: do you know where AI is used, who is accountable and how risks are tested? A full programme is overkill for a forty-person company, but a lightweight version is achievable and useful.

**Our take.** Four moves cover most of the value. First, **Govern**: name an owner and write a one-page acceptable-use policy covering what data may go into which tools. Second, **Map**: keep an inventory of AI tools and use cases with a risk tier for each, such as low (internal drafting), medium (customer-facing but reviewed) and high (automated decisions affecting people). Third, **Measure**: for medium and high tiers, define tests such as accuracy, bias and adversarial checks. Fourth, **Manage**: agree how issues are reported, who can switch a system off and when you review it.

**What to do this week.** Start the inventory in a spreadsheet and ask each team lead what AI tools they actually use. The answers are usually surprising.

ISO/IEC 42001, published in 2023, specifies requirements for an artificial intelligence management system (AIMS). Like ISO 27001 for information security, it is a management-system standard: it asks you to define scope, assess risk, set policies, assign responsibilities, monitor performance and improve, and an accredited body can audit and certify you against it.

**Why it matters for Indian businesses.** Indian AI product companies and service providers selling into regulated markets may soon see customers ask for a recognised assurance signal. Procurement teams like certificates because they are easy to compare, though a certificate says only that a management system exists and works, not that every model is safe or accurate.

**Our take.** If you build AI products for enterprise buyers, or operate AI in sensitive domains, start reading the standard and mapping it onto work you already do. If you only use off-the-shelf AI tools internally, a short policy and tool inventory is enough for now. Companies already certified to ISO 27001 have a head start because both standards share structure, so one integrated system is cheaper than two separate ones.

**What to do this week.** Check whether any customer questionnaire or tender has mentioned AI governance. If so, ask what evidence they will accept. Then compare your current practices to the standard's clauses to see the size of the gap.

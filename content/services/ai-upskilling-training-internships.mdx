## The problem: AI adoption without skills

Many organisations bought AI tools before they trained anyone to use them. Some staff avoid them out of caution; others paste confidential data into public chatbots; a few use them brilliantly and nobody learns from them. Developers copy generated code without understanding its security implications. Students graduate with theoretical knowledge but no experience shipping anything.

Training works when it changes what people do on Monday morning. Ours is built around that.

## Programmes for organisations

**AI for everyone (half day to one day).** How language models work in plain terms, where they fail, safe data-handling rules, good prompting habits, workflows for writing, analysis and research, and how to verify output. Tailored for managers, HR, sales, marketing and operations.

**AI for developers (two to three days).** Using code assistants responsibly, building LLM features with retrieval and tool use, evaluating outputs, handling errors and cost, and testing for prompt injection. Participants build a working assistant over a sample dataset and test it for faults.

**Secure coding workshop (one to two days).** The OWASP Top 10 and API risks through hands-on exercises: find and fix injection, broken access control and insecure deserialisation in a sample application, plus threat modelling and review checklists that fit your pull-request process.

**Security awareness for staff (one to two hours).** Phishing, credential hygiene, safe handling of customer data, AI-tool rules and what to do when something looks wrong, using real examples relevant to Indian businesses, including UPI and invoice fraud patterns.

**AI governance for leaders (half day).** Risk, policy and oversight for executives and managers, linking to our [AI governance](/services/ai-security-governance) work.

## Internships and early-career programme

Our internships are deliberately small. Interns are placed with a delivery team on a scoped project, such as a feature for an internal tool, a test automation suite, a security checklist or an evaluation set for an AI assistant. Each intern has a mentor, takes part in code or report reviews, and presents their work at the end. We aim for a portfolio piece, not a certificate printed from a template.

Eligibility, duration, stipend and number of places vary by cohort and are announced on this page and on our [careers page](/careers). We do not charge fees for internships, and we are wary of any programme that sells jobs.

## How we design a programme

1. **Needs conversation.** Roles, current skills, tools, data sensitivity and what outcomes you want.
2. **Custom curriculum.** We build exercises around your stack and use cases, not generic slides.
3. **Delivery.** In person at your Hyderabad office, at ours in Kukatpally or online, in small groups so everyone gets feedback.
4. **Assessment.** Practical tasks during and after the programme to show what has been learned.
5. **Follow-up.** Office hours, a resource pack and an optional check-in a few weeks later to see what stuck.

## Tools and approach

Exercises use a mix of language-model APIs, open-source libraries, intentionally vulnerable applications for secure-coding practice, Git workflows and your own tools where permitted. We keep sample data synthetic unless you provide sanitised data, and instructors are practitioners who work on live [engineering](/services/custom-software-development), [security](/services/vapt-penetration-testing) and [AI](/services/ai-agents-rag-assistants) projects.

## Measuring whether training worked

We agree outcomes before we start: for a secure-coding workshop, perhaps fewer injection and access-control findings in the next penetration test; for AI training, a shared set of approved workflows and a drop in unapproved tool use. Participants complete a short practical task at the end and again a few weeks later. You receive a plain summary of what improved and what still needs coaching, so training becomes an investment you can evaluate rather than a calendar event, and each cohort improves on the last.

## The problem: you cannot see your own blind spots

Developers test that features work. Attackers test what happens when they do not. The gap between those two mindsets is where breaches live: an API that returns another customer's invoice when an ID is changed, a mobile app that stores tokens in plain text, an S3 bucket that was made public for a quick test and never closed, an admin panel reachable from the internet with a default password.

Automated scanners catch some of this, but they cannot understand your business logic. They will not notice that a discount code can be applied unlimited times, or that the password reset flow leaks whether an email exists. Finding those issues takes a human tester working through your application the way an adversary would.

## Our approach to VAPT

We treat testing as an engineering service, not a PDF factory. Every engagement starts with scoping: what the system does, who uses it, what an attacker would want and what must never be touched during testing. We then combine automated discovery with manual testing, because automation gives breadth and manual work gives depth.

Findings are reproducible. Each one includes the affected endpoint or asset, step-by-step reproduction, evidence such as requests and screenshots, a business-impact explanation, a severity rating with the reasoning shown, and specific remediation guidance your developers can act on. We rate with CVSS and adjust for your context, and we explain the adjustment instead of hiding it.

## What we test

- **Web applications and APIs:** authentication, session handling, access control (including IDOR and privilege escalation), injection, business logic, file handling, server-side request forgery and client-side issues.
- **Mobile applications (Android and iOS):** insecure storage, certificate pinning and transport security, exported components, reverse-engineering resistance and API abuse from the app.
- **Network and infrastructure:** external and internal exposure, misconfigurations, weak services, segmentation and Active Directory attack paths where in scope.
- **Cloud environments (AWS, Azure, GCP):** identity and access management, storage exposure, network rules, secrets, logging gaps and container or Kubernetes configuration.
- **Secure code review:** manual and tool-assisted review of critical code paths such as authentication, payments and data access, with findings tied to the exact lines and a fix suggestion.

## Deliverables

1. Scoping document and rules of engagement signed before testing starts.
2. Executive summary written for leadership, with risk themes and priorities.
3. Detailed technical report with reproducible findings and remediation advice.
4. A working session with your developers to walk through the findings.
5. One retest round and a closure letter listing what is fixed and what remains.

## Process

1. **Scope and plan.** Agree targets, credentials, test accounts, constraints and timelines.
2. **Reconnaissance and mapping.** Enumerate the attack surface, technologies and trust boundaries.
3. **Vulnerability identification.** Combine scanning with manual probing of each feature.
4. **Exploitation.** Validate impact safely, chain issues and demonstrate realistic attack paths without causing damage.
5. **Reporting.** Deliver prioritised, actionable findings within an agreed turnaround.
6. **Remediation support and retest.** Answer developer questions and verify fixes.

## Tools and references

Our testers use Burp Suite, Nmap, Metasploit, MobSF, Frida, Semgrep, ScoutSuite or Prowler for cloud posture and custom scripts, but tools are only as good as the person using them, so the report always states what was verified manually. Methodology references include OWASP WSTG, OWASP ASVS, OWASP MASVS and MASTG, PTES, NIST SP 800-115 and CIS Benchmarks. We do not claim individual tester certifications on this page until they are verified.

## Preparing for a test

You can get more value from any engagement with a little preparation: provide test accounts for each role, share API documentation or a Postman collection, tell us about any third-party systems that must not be touched, and nominate a developer who can answer questions during testing. Tell us what keeps you awake, such as account takeover or data export abuse, and we will prioritise those paths first.

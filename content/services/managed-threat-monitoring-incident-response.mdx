## The problem: logs nobody reads

Most companies already collect security data: cloud audit trails, firewall logs, endpoint alerts, email gateway reports. The problem is that nobody has time to look at it. Alerts pile up, important ones hide among noise, and the first sign of an intrusion is a customer complaint, a ransom note or an unexpected invoice. When an incident does happen, teams discover they do not know who decides to shut down a server, who calls the lawyer, or where the logs are.

Managed monitoring and incident response exists to fix both halves: noticing early, and responding calmly.

## What we offer

**Managed threat monitoring.** We connect to your identity provider, endpoints, email, cloud accounts, network devices and key applications, tune detections to your environment, and triage alerts so your team receives a small number of enriched, actionable cases instead of raw noise.

**Incident response retainer.** A pre-agreed arrangement with response hours, contacts, severity definitions and runbooks, so when something happens the first call is already planned.

**Incident readiness.** Tabletop exercises, runbook development, logging gap assessments and backup and recovery reviews, so that the next incident costs less.

**Emergency response.** Containment, investigation and recovery support for active incidents, including business email compromise, ransomware, credential theft, and compromised cloud keys.

## Our approach

We begin with an assessment: which assets matter, what logs you have, what you are missing and which threats are realistic for your sector. Detection coverage is mapped to the MITRE ATT&CK framework so you can see which attacker behaviours you can and cannot currently detect, and gaps become a prioritised roadmap.

Detection content is tuned against your own baseline. A rule that fires fifty times a day teaches people to ignore it, so we measure false-positive rates and retire or rewrite noisy rules. Every alert we escalate includes what happened, why we think it matters, what we checked and what we recommend, so the person reading it at 2am can decide in minutes.

## Engagement process

1. **Assess:** assets, data, existing logs, regulatory duties.
2. **Onboard:** connect sources, validate data quality, set retention.
3. **Tune:** build and test detections, remove noise, agree escalation rules.
4. **Operate:** continuous monitoring in the agreed coverage window, with defined response times.
5. **Respond:** follow the runbook during incidents, with evidence handling suitable for later legal or regulatory use.
6. **Improve:** monthly reviews, quarterly tabletop exercises and detection updates after every incident.

## Tools and standards

We favour tools you can own: open or commercial SIEM and log platforms, endpoint detection and response from your existing vendor, cloud-native services such as AWS CloudTrail and GuardDuty or Microsoft Defender and Sentinel, and open-source forensic utilities. Process references include NIST SP 800-61 for incident handling, MITRE ATT&CK for detection coverage and the CERT-In directions on incident reporting and log retention. We keep monitoring data in your tenant wherever possible so you are not locked in.

## Regulatory and reporting support

Indian organisations have specific timelines. CERT-In's directions of April 2022 call for reporting specified incident types within six hours and for retaining certain logs within India for a defined period, and sector regulators add their own rules. The DPDP Act adds duties to notify the Data Protection Board and affected individuals of personal data breaches. We help you map these obligations to a runbook so decisions during a crisis are quick, and we recommend legal counsel for formal interpretation.

## The problem: lots of activity, little pipeline

Most B2B marketing teams are busy. They publish blog posts, post on social channels, run campaigns and send newsletters. Yet when sales asks where qualified leads come from, the answer is vague. Content is written for keywords nobody buys with, the website is slow or poorly structured for search, social posting is disconnected from product messaging and reports celebrate traffic that never turned into conversations.

Good content marketing is closer to product work than to publishing. It starts from buyers' real questions, builds a body of work that answers them better than competitors do, makes that work easy for search engines and people to find, and measures its effect on pipeline.

## Our approach

**Start with buyers, not keywords.** We interview sales and customers, read support tickets and sales call notes, and map the questions people ask at each stage from problem awareness to vendor selection. Keyword research then tells us how those questions are searched, not what to care about.

**Build topic clusters, not isolated posts.** A strong pillar page, supported by detailed articles that link to it and to each other, gives search engines and readers a clear picture of what you know. We plan clusters around your commercial services, as we do on this site, so every article has a job.

**Edit for experience and evidence.** Search engines and buyers both reward content that shows first-hand knowledge: real examples, specific numbers you can source, named authors with credentials and clear dates. We never invent statistics, testimonials or case studies, and we mark anything uncertain for verification.

**Fix the technical foundation.** Content cannot rank if pages are not crawled, indexed and fast. Technical SEO is half of the work for many sites.

**Measure what sales cares about.** We tie each content and campaign effort to qualified enquiries in your CRM, so decisions follow pipeline rather than vanity metrics.

## Process

1. **Discover (2 to 3 weeks).** Interviews, data review, technical audit and competitor analysis.
2. **Plan.** Positioning, clusters, calendar, technical roadmap and measurement plan.
3. **Produce and fix.** Content and technical fixes released in a steady rhythm, with editorial review.
4. **Distribute.** Search, email, social and partner channels, planned with each piece rather than after it.
5. **Measure.** Monthly reports covering indexed pages, impressions, qualified leads and what we will change next.
6. **Refine.** Quarterly strategy reviews, content refreshes and experiments.
